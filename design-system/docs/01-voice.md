# Voice

Write like a practitioner: calm, exact, a little playful, and never longer than the thought. Words are as fixed as the greens.

## Who the words are for

- **The engineer becoming an agentic engineer.** Show the craft: real code, real checks, the engineer directing and verifying. Never promise that the agent does the job.
- **The entrepreneurial lead, ready to be paid for results, not hours.** Show the outcome and the arithmetic, and make the relationship worth starting.

## Five qualities

- **Professional.** Exact nouns and checked numbers. A boast becomes a fact or goes.
- **A little playful.** One small image or a turn of rhythm. No puns, no exclamation marks. "This page is a cocoon."
- **Invites experiment.** Low-stakes verbs: try, sketch, break, undo.
- **Grounded.** The engineer is the subject. Agents are directed and checked, never partners or minds; the engineer's checks decide when the work is done.
- **Inspiring.** Name the after-state, not the tool. "In six weeks you build it into how your team works."

Never write: revolutionise, 10x, magic, supercharge, autonomy as a selling point, or a wellbeing promise.

## The tagline

"Curious play. Epic growth." decodes the name: PLEPIC is PL(ay) plus EPIC. Never reword it and never translate it. Set it in Bitter italic 500 in `green-brand` when it has room; in the footer it is quiet ink that warms to `green-brand` on hover. It is the one line allowed to be green throughout.

Estonian copy carries its own line instead, "Tulemuspõhine digimuutus". It names the outcome for a buyer who must justify a spend. Never present it as a translation of the tagline.

## The category line

What Plepic sells: "{{category_line}}". Use these exact words.

## The headline pattern

A heading is `text`, and at most one phrase inside it is `green-brand`. The green phrase is the claim: remove the green and the heading still reads.

- Do: "**Practitioners**, not trainers" with only "Practitioners" in `green-brand`.
- Don't: the whole heading in green. It emphasises nothing and leaves the next green phrase nothing to earn.

## Rules that travel

These hold in a heading, a slide, an email and a proposal.

- No em-dashes in anything a customer reads.
- Write "cohort", never "squad".
- Sentence, then proof: the supporting line sits in `text-2`. If it is not quieter, the heading is not carrying.
- A rewrite earns its music by losing words. It never outgrows the line it replaces.
- Keep volatile values out of design material: prices and discounts, cohort dates and deadlines, seat counts, engineers trained, ratings, and names of people who have not signed. They live on the page that states them.
