Section 5

Strategic note: the two moves Google Fonts cannot provide

License a characterful foundry slab or serif

Faces in the Jubilat, Sentinel, or Caponi lane carry Clarendon-grade character that no competitor stumbles into via a free-fonts search, which is exactly the ownability the Zilla problem exposes. The cost is real: web licenses are typically annual and priced by traffic, and every renewal is a dependency on the foundry. Self-hosting on a static site is straightforward though: two woff2 files and an @font-face block, no CDN, usually faster than Google Fonts.

Customize 2 or 3 letterforms of the wordmark only

A lettering pass on “Plepic”, say the two P bowls and the c terminal, turns the wordmark from a font rendering into owned artwork: shipped as SVG beside the butterfly, zero font dependency, one-time cost. It is the cheapest genuine ownability on this page, and it decouples identity from typography: the display face can change later, or never, without touching the mark. The risk is small and known: amateur lettering reads instantly, so this is a commission for a type designer, not an in-house tweak.
