Plepic · Design system

Voice

The words are as fixed as the greens. A tagline that decodes the name and only works in English, an Estonian line that stands in its place, a category line that says what is sold, and one headline pattern. Everything else is written fresh, against the rules at the bottom.

The tagline, and why it is those four words

It is the name's decoder ring. PLEPIC is PL(ay) plus EPIC, and each half of the tagline names one half of the name. That is the whole reason it cannot be reworded.

PL(ay)EPIC

"Curious play" names the PL. "Epic growth" names the EPIC. Change either half and the name stops explaining itself.

Curious play. Epic growth.

Bitter italic 500 in --green-brand when it is given room. In the footer it sits at 0.75rem in the quieter ink and warms to green on hover, which is the only easter egg on the site. It is a tagline, not a heading, so it is the one line allowed to be green all the way through.

What Estonian carries instead

The wordplay lives inside an English name, so the tagline only works in English. Estonian does not attempt it and does not translate it; it carries a different line that does a different job.

EN

Curious play. Epic growth.

The tagline is the line. Nothing stands in for it, because nothing else decodes the name.

ET

Tulemuspõhine digimuutus

Names the outcome, because an Estonian buyer is justifying a spend and the pun is unavailable. Never a translation of the tagline, and never presented as one.

The category line

Separate from both of the above, and the only one of the three that says what Plepic sells. It is the training page’s own headline, so it can be checked against a live page rather than against a design file.

Agentic engineering for dev teams

Engineering, not coding: the training is about directing agents to ship production work, which is more than writing code. It also shows the headline pattern doing its job, with green on the phrase the page is about.

The headline pattern

A heading is ink, and at most one phrase inside it carries green. The green phrase is the claim, not the decoration.

Practitioners, not trainers

Green marks the word the page is about; ink carries the qualification. Take the green away and the heading still reads, which is the test.

Never this

Practitioners, not trainers

A fully green heading spends the brand colour on a whole sentence, so nothing inside it is emphasised and the next green phrase on the page has nothing left to earn.

Rules that travel

They hold in a heading, a slide, an email and a proposal. None of them is a house style; each one prevents a specific failure.

No em-dashesNot in anything a customer reads. Commas, colons and full stops carry the same joins and survive every mail client.
Cohort, not squadThe word changed on 2026-08-31. Older records keep the old one; nothing new does.
One green phrasePer heading, and never the whole of it. Green is a claim, not a colour scheme.
Sentence, then proofThe supporting line is set in the quieter ink. If it is not quieter, the heading is not carrying.

What never enters this canvas. Volatile values live in the page that states them, so one edit changes one place and the claims gate can see it. If you need one, read it off the live page, never off a design file.

  • Prices, discounts and any figure with a currency on it
  • Cohort dates, deadlines and anything counted in weeks from today
  • Seat counts, developers trained, ratings, any number that grows
  • Names of people who have not signed
