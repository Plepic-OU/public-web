Instructor card, six directions

Same three people, same words, same photographs. The only variable is the design. Every direction drops the triangulated portraits, the shard backdrop and the black edges, and every one keeps the lift and the tilt, so hover them. Each carries a note on what it chose and why.

Quiet panel

The instructor card is not a new object: it is one of the site's own panels with a person in it, and its authority comes from belonging rather than from shouting. The bet is that once the chrome is gone, the name, the hairline and the photograph waking up under the pointer are enough.

Editorial rule

The card stops being a box and becomes a column of a printed page, held by two horizontal rules and nothing else. It bets that the paper canon means paper, and that on a page like this the quietest treatment is the one that reads as the most confident.

Resting elevation is nothing at all: no border, no shadow, no radius and no tint under the column, so it is printed on the page rather than placed on it, and its only edges are a 1.5px ink rule at the top and a 1px --border rule at the foot. Hover fades in a single sheet of --surface behind the column and raises it 5px on a wide soft shadow, 0 10px 28px rgba(28,28,26,0.08) over a 1px contact edge, so elevation stays a state; the tilt caps drop to 9 and 11 degrees on a 1400px perspective because paper flexes less than card stock. The photographs are real and untouched in shape: a square plate of --bg-alt with the shot multiplied onto it in one grey ink, which puts three pictures taken on three different days on one ground and in one tone without a single cut-out. Literals the system has no token for, all named in the CSS where they sit: the 1.5px rule weight, the 24px sheet bleed, the 1.4rem byline, the 0.16em mono tracking, the 5px lift and its shadow, the three tilt values, and the per-person crop numbers that put the three heads at one size with the eyes on one line. The type line and the foot plate both stay, because a contributor column is exactly the place where a standing head and a colophon earn their keep.

Stamp

The photograph is a print mounted inside a margin, not a picture the card is cut from, so the air around a face carries as much of the formality as the face does. This bets that a measured window and a wide mount make three people read as one issued set, where three edge-to-edge photographs read as three snapshots.

The photographs are untouched colour, no filter and no duotone, because the set is made by geometry alone: each frame is scaled until the heads match and shifted until every head top lands on one line, which the render holds across the three to within half a percentage point of the window height. The duotone is two blends, not a filter: the portrait's grayscale screened over a green shadow ink, then the paper cream multiplied over that, which composes to the same straight gradient map an feComponentTransfer would give. It is built that way because the two tones stay spelled as var(--green-dark) and var(--bg-alt) where the palette guard can read them, because filter: url() needs a document-unique id and six comps share one page, and because CSS filters work in sRGB where feColorMatrix quietly defaults to linearRGB. The edge is a 1px var(--border) hairline; the card rests on the canon's smallest shadow, 0 2px 8px rgba(28,28,26,0.04), and on hover lifts by var(--lift-height) onto the canon's hover lift, 0 8px 24px rgba(28,28,26,0.06), while the print dissolves and the photograph arrives in full colour. Two things are literals because no token holds them: rgba(28,28,26,0.42) knocks var(--green-dark) back into an ink deep enough that skin reads sage rather than neon, and each portrait carries its own scale, its own contrast and, for the one file low enough in key to need it, a lift in exposure, so three head sizes and three exposures land on one tonal curve. The foot plate is gone, because PLEPIC · INSTRUCTOR repeats the word the type line above already says, and a card this plain cannot afford furniture that says nothing twice.

Standing figure

No window, no frame: the person is cut out and stands directly on a flat brand-green field that runs off all four edges of the card. The bet is that a real photograph standing in a coloured space carries the whole card, and that the green does the identity work the shards were doing badly.

The photograph is the card: it runs the full 5:7 bleed, no border is drawn at all, and the edge is held instead by the picture and by a lit backdrop that cools from white behind the face to the green surface tint at the corners. Every source is head and shoulders on a ground that measures pure white, and a head and shoulders shot cannot fill a tall card at any crop that keeps the head, so the card supplies the missing backdrop and the photograph is composited in multiply, which dissolves each source's own white into that field exactly and leaves no seam; each portrait is then enlarged and offset on its own three percentages, because the three were shot at three different distances and one crop would stand three different sized heads in a row. Shot properly, head to waist on a seamless coloured backdrop, none of that would be needed: the ground could be a flat brand colour, the blend would come out, and Vootele's 512px file would stop being visibly the softest of the three. Elevation replaces the hard ink offset that went with the border with two soft ones, a 0 1px 2px contact shadow and a 0 8px 18px ambient shadow, both rgba tints of --text, opening to 0 2px 4px and 0 24px 44px on hover over the shipped 12px lift and the shipped tilt. The foot plate is dropped because no footer is left for it to sit in, and the type that stays sits on rgba(250, 247, 242, 0.90) over a 12px backdrop blur, measured on screen, column by column, at 13.0:1 to 15.7:1 for the name and 6.8:1 at its worst for the two secondary lines; the 5:7 ratio, the plate alpha, the blur radius, the shadow geometry, the nine crop percentages and the three gradient centres are all literals, because the system carries a token for none of them.
