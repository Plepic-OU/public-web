What a performance-based price rests on

Measurement is one condition of five. Kaido’s master’s research at the University of Tartu maps the rest, and the order: each tier has to hold up the one above it.

A real deal, anonymized

Operations software for a logistics company, priced on results

Offer
Fixed fees cover the build. On top: 30% of verified savings, quarterly, for 12 months. At target, ~€45K a year.
Baseline
The metric and its starting level are certified in writing before go-live; savings are computed from the client’s own operating data.
Guardrails
Only results confirmed by the client’s own team count, and only while a paired quality metric holds its baseline.
Delay
Every month before go-live leaves ~€12K of savings unclaimed.
Team
The fee is a pool the delivery team shares. On hourly billing, finishing early means billing less; here it moves a metric with a price on it.

On revenue deals the same structure runs over a certified baseline; the client picks the risk balance between fixed fee and share (Koort, 2025). Where no outcome is measurable, the proposal says so and recommends fixed or hourly instead.
