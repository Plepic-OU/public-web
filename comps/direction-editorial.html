Resting elevation is nothing at all: no border, no shadow, no radius and no tint under the column, so it is printed on the page rather than placed on it, and its only edges are a 1.5px ink rule at the top and a 1px --border rule at the foot. Hover fades in a single sheet of --surface behind the column and raises it 5px on a wide soft shadow, 0 10px 28px rgba(28,28,26,0.08) over a 1px contact edge, so elevation stays a state; the tilt caps drop to 9 and 11 degrees on a 1400px perspective because paper flexes less than card stock. The photographs are real and untouched in shape: a square plate of --bg-alt with the shot multiplied onto it in one grey ink, which puts three pictures taken on three different days on one ground and in one tone without a single cut-out. Literals the system has no token for, all named in the CSS where they sit: the 1.5px rule weight, the 24px sheet bleed, the 1.4rem byline, the 0.16em mono tracking, the 5px lift and its shadow, the three tilt values, and the per-person crop numbers that put the three heads at one size with the eyes on one line. The type line and the foot plate both stay, because a contributor column is exactly the place where a standing head and a colophon earn their keep.
