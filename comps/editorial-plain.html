The filter is none: same plate, same --bg-alt ground, same multiply, same per-person crops, so the one variable between this comp and the base is the ink the photographs are printed in. The ground and the faces survive that: all three plates still measure exactly 243,239,231 at every sample point with a single-pixel step from ground to subject, the same as the base, so multiply keeps one seamless ground and colour costs it nothing (contrast(1.08) on top moved that figure by under a percentage point, so the base's press setting is not here either), and the three cheeks land inside H14 to H18 at S0.22 to S0.39, one skin family, warmed a few points in the blue channel by the cream multiplying up into them. The garments do not survive it, and the break is Joosep beside Kaido: a yellow shirt at H49 S0.52 V0.87 in the next column to a jersey at H191 S1.00 V0.47, 142 degrees apart at the two highest chromas anywhere on the page, both in the bottom third of the plate where the eye lands after the name, and that jersey is a fully saturated cyan sitting under a butterfly whose green is H137. Mean chroma per plate is 0.17, 0.33 and 0.22 against the base's 0.05, 0.05 and 0.05, which is what one ink meant, and the plate is now the loudest object in a column whose whole argument is that the byline leads; at 390 the row stacks, no two garments are ever on screen together and the clash all but goes away, while at 820 the row goes two up and puts those same two alone and larger, which is where it is worst, so this is a row problem and not a photograph problem.
