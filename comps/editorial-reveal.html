At rest the photographs are the base untouched, one grey ink multiplied onto a warm --bg-alt plate with one contrast on all three; hover fades that to colour on the hovered card alone and the blend stays on, so the plate's warm tint keeps multiplying back into the picture, roughly 5% off the reds and 9% off the blues, which is the only thing putting three rooms on one cast. The timing is the site's own scale played fastest first: the standing head over --dur-fast, the sheet and the lift over --dur-base, and the face waiting out --dur-fast before it takes --dur-settle on --ease-settle, so the reveal lands at 700ms, which is --dur-entrance; the exit drains over --dur-base on --ease-calm, because ease-out-expo on the way out strips the colour in three frames and reads as a switch rather than a face going back to sleep. Honestly, forced into colour side by side the three do not agree: the skin and the grounds match, but a canary shirt beside a teal jersey is a different room and no tint hides it, and this is the one variant where that frame is never rendered, because a card is in colour only while it is being looked at. Touch has no hover to earn it with, so it gets colour permanently and a notch quieter, at saturate(0.78), which holds on a phone where the row is one column and the three are never side by side, and fails on a tablet wide enough for two, the one frame here I would not defend. Literals the system has no token for: the hover grade contrast(1.06) brightness(1.04) saturate(0.9), the touch grade contrast(1.08) brightness(1.01) saturate(0.78), and the 18px under the day-job line where the deleted colophon's rule now sits.
