Duotone

The card is plain on purpose: a hairline, the smallest shadow in the canon, and nothing else. This direction bets that three portraits look like a set when they are printed in one ink on one paper, and that no frame can do that job for them.

The duotone is two blends, not a filter: the portrait's grayscale screened over a green shadow ink, then the paper cream multiplied over that, which composes to the same straight gradient map an feComponentTransfer would give. It is built that way because the two tones stay spelled as var(--green-dark) and var(--bg-alt) where the palette guard can read them, because filter: url() needs a document-unique id and six comps share one page, and because CSS filters work in sRGB where feColorMatrix quietly defaults to linearRGB. The edge is a 1px var(--border) hairline; the card rests on the canon's smallest shadow, 0 2px 8px rgba(28,28,26,0.04), and on hover lifts by var(--lift-height) onto the canon's hover lift, 0 8px 24px rgba(28,28,26,0.06), while the print dissolves and the photograph arrives in full colour. Two things are literals because no token holds them: rgba(28,28,26,0.42) knocks var(--green-dark) back into an ink deep enough that skin reads sage rather than neon, and each portrait carries its own scale, its own contrast and, for the one file low enough in key to need it, a lift in exposure, so three head sizes and three exposures land on one tonal curve. The foot plate is gone, because PLEPIC · INSTRUCTOR repeats the word the type line above already says, and a card this plain cannot afford furniture that says nothing twice.
